| Target | Value | Why |

## 2. How to make this work in an Indian family kitchen

1. **Buy a ₹300–500 digital kitchen scale.** Weigh your own portions for the first 3–4 weeks. After that, you'll know what your katori, ladle and roti look like and only need to spot-check.
2. **Cooking oil is the #1 hidden reason Indian diets stall.** One extra tablespoon of oil or ghee is **~120 kcal**. Across three meals, untracked oil easily adds 400–600 kcal and wipes out most of your deficit.
   - **Rule for your portions:** ~1 teaspoon (5 g) of oil per meal, **~20–25 g oil/ghee total for the whole day**.
   - **Practical family fix:** don't ask your family to change how they cook for everyone. Ask them to **scoop your dal or sabzi out before the final heavy tadka/ghee**, or cook your eggs and chicken in a separate non-stick pan with a measured teaspoon of oil.
3. **Standard conversions used in the plan**
   - **1 home phulka/roti** (medium, ~6 inch, no ghee) ≈ **30 g dry atta** = 102 kcal, 3.6 g P, 21 g C. If your family's rotis are larger (40–45 g atta), count 2 of theirs as 3 of mine.
   - **150 g cooked rice** ≈ 50 g raw rice ≈ 1.5 standard katoris = 195 kcal, 4 g P, 42 g C.
   - **Dal / rajma / chole** are listed by **dry weight** because families add different amounts of water. **30 g dry dal ≈ 1 thick katori cooked** (~102 kcal, 7 g P). If the family dal is watery, take the thick scoop from the bottom.
   - **Chicken and fish** are listed by **raw weight** (150 g raw chicken breast ≈ 110–115 g cooked).
   - **Hung curd:** pour toned curd into a muslin cloth or fine strainer over a bowl in the fridge for 3–4 hours. It costs a third of commercial Greek yogurt and gives ~8 g protein per 100 g.
4. **Spices, ginger, garlic, green chillies, coriander, lemon, vinegar, mustard:** use freely. Count ~50 kcal/day as a buffer for trace masala calories (already absorbed by the deficit).

## 4. Swap tables (mix and match without breaking your macros)

### 4.1 Protein swaps (each gives ≈ 25–30 g protein)

| Food | Quantity | Approx. kcal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast (raw) | 125 g | 150 | Leanest, easiest way to hit protein |
| Fish — rohu, katla, surmai, bangda (raw) | 150 g | 135–220 | Bangda (mackerel) has more fat, which is omega-3 |
| Egg whites + whole eggs | 5 whites + 1 whole | 155 | Or 8 egg whites (~140 kcal). Give extra yolks to family. |
| Soya chunks / granules (dry) | 50 g | 173 (16 g C) | Boil, squeeze out water, then cook in masala. Cheapest protein in India. |
| Whey protein | 1 scoop (30 g) | 120 | Convenience only |
| Low-fat / milky-mist paneer | 120 g | 220–260 | Check label |
| Regular full-fat paneer | 140 g | **~390 (31 g fat)** | High calorie. On paneer days, drop 1 roti or 1 tsp oil elsewhere. |
| Hung toned curd | 350 g | 340 | Good before bed |
| Dal / rajma / chole (dry) | 110 g | **~375 (65 g C)** | This is a **carb + protein** source, not pure protein |

### 4.2 Vegetarian swaps for any non-veg meal (festival weeks, Shravan, Navratri)
- **Replace 150 g raw chicken breast (180 kcal, 34 g P)** with any of:
  - **65 g dry soya chunks** (224 kcal, 34 g P) *and* cut ½ roti from the meal.
  - **1 scoop whey (30 g) + 150 g hung curd** (265 kcal, 36 g P) *and* cut ½ roti.
  - **100 g paneer + 30 g dry soya granules** (380 kcal, 34 g P) *and* cut 1 roti + 1 tsp oil.
- **Replace the morning eggs (3 whole + 3 whites ≈ 265 kcal, 30 g P)** with:
  - **60 g besan or moong-dal chilla + 150 g hung curd + ½ scoop whey**, or
  - **50 g soya-granule bhurji + 200 ml toned milk + 10 g peanuts**.

### 4.3 Carb swaps (each ≈ 20–22 g carbs, ~100 kcal)
1 roti (30 g atta) **=** 75 g cooked rice **=** 30 g dry oats **=** 30 g dry poha **=** 1 medium banana (100 g) **=** 120 g boiled potato **=** 1.5 slices brown bread.

### 4.4 Campus and eating-out emergency swaps
Tandoori chicken (half ≈ 500 kcal, 65 g P; skip the butter brush) · paneer tikka (6 pcs ≈ 450 kcal, 28 g P) · 4 boiled eggs from a thela (280 kcal, 24 g P) · roasted chana packet 50 g (185 kcal, 10 g P) · buttermilk/chaas + Amul protein lassi/buttermilk (15 g P, ₹25–30).

## 6. Fat-loss strategy

### 6.1 Weekly rate of loss
- **Target:** **0.5–0.9% of bodyweight per week** in Phases 1–2 (≈ 0.55–1.0 kg/wk now), slowing to **0.4–0.7%/wk** in Phases 3–4 (≈ 0.4–0.65 kg/wk at 92 kg).
- **Why not faster?** Faster loss (> 1% BW/week for weeks on end) increases lean-mass loss, tanks gym performance and spikes hunger.
- **Exception:** Week 1–2 often drops **1.5–3 kg** because glycogen and its bound water leave first. Ignore that when judging your rate.

### 6.2 How to track (and not get fooled by water)
Weigh every morning, log it in the tracker, and **only ever react to the 7-day average compared across 2 weeks**.
Single-day scale jumps of 0.5–2 kg come from:
- A salty dinner or restaurant meal (water retained for 24–48 h).
- A higher-carb meal (each gram of glycogen holds ~3 g of water).
- Heavy leg training (inflammation water during repair).
- Bowel content and sleep timing.

### 6.3 When and how to cut calories
Wait until **two consecutive weekly averages** lose less than **0.4% BW/week** *and* your waist hasn't dropped *and* your adherence was ≥ 90%. Then:
- **Cut 150 kcal/day** by removing **2 rotis (or 150 g cooked rice)** *or* **1 roti + 1 tsp oil**.
- **Never cut protein.** Keep fat ≥ 60 g/day.

### 6.4 Managing hunger (becomes important from Phase 2 onward)
1. **Eat a big salad or soup before lunch and dinner:** 150–200 g cucumber, tomato, radish, carrot or लौकी/तोरई/पत्तागोभी sabzi. High volume, ~30–50 kcal.
2. **Swap rice for boiled potatoes** on hungry days (potatoes score highest on the satiety index per calorie).
3. **Protein and fibre in every meal** (already built into the plan).
4. **Solid food beats liquid:** if the pre-workout shake stops filling you up in Phase 3, stir the whey into oats or hung curd and eat it with a spoon.
5. **500 ml water** 15 minutes before each main meal.
6. **Zero/low-calorie drinks:** nimbu pani with salt/stevia, chaas, black coffee, unsweetened green tea, diet soda in moderation.
7. **Sleep 7.5–9 h:** short sleep raises ghrelin and makes you noticeably hungrier the next day.

### 6.5 Avoiding muscle loss
Keep these five in place and muscle loss will be minimal:
1. **Hard progressive lifting** (loads on the bar maintained or rising).
2. **Protein 170–190 g/day**, every day, including rest and exam days.
3. **Rate of loss ≤ 1% BW/week.**
4. **Sleep ≥ 7.5 h.**
5. **No crash cuts or dehydration tricks.**

### 6.6 Planned diet breaks (Weeks 12, 24, 36, 48 — matched with deload weeks)
For 7 days, raise calories by **+400–600 kcal/day** (to your current estimated maintenance), almost all from **carbs** (+3–4 rotis or +250 g cooked rice + 1 fruit across the day). Protein stays at 180 g.
- Expect the scale to bounce **+1–2 kg** from glycogen and water. It drops back within a week of resuming the deficit.
- Diet breaks restore training performance, reduce mental fatigue and let you practice eating at maintenance, which is how you'll keep the weight off after Month 12.

### 6.7 Intermittent fasting: useful or necessary?
**Not necessary.** Across controlled trials where calories and protein are matched, intermittent fasting produces **the same fat loss** as regular meal timing.
For you specifically, squeezing 180 g of Indian protein into an 8-hour window is uncomfortable, and training hard or walking 18k steps on an empty stomach can hurt performance. **Stick with 4–5 meals spread across ~12–13 hours** (e.g. 8 am to 9 pm). The only "fasting" rule worth keeping is stopping food ~1–2 hours before sleep so digestion doesn't disturb your sleep.
