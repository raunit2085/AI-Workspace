## 1. Why this split
**Push / Pull / Legs × 2, with Sunday off.**
- **Every muscle trained 2×/week.** When total weekly sets are the same, training a muscle twice a week builds at least as much muscle as once a week, and it lets you spread hard sets across sessions so every set is higher quality.
- **Side delts 3×/week, lats and upper back 2×/week.** The V-taper muscles get the most frequency and volume.
- **Leg volume is moderate on purpose.** You already walk ~13 km a day. Your legs need *heavy, progressive* work, not junk volume that eats into recovery during a deficit.
- **Fits your 80-minute sessions:** 6–8 exercises and 17–20 hard sets per session.
- **Not an advanced bodybuilder split.** No 25-set chest days, no drop-set marathons. You'll get more from a smaller number of high-quality sets you can recover from.

### Weekly layout

| Day | Session | Focus | After lifting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Push A | Bench strength, upper chest, side delts, triceps, abs | Incline walk 20 min |
| **Tue** | Pull A | Lat width, rows, rear delts, biceps, neck | Incline walk 20 min |
| **Wed** | Legs A | Squat, quads, hamstrings (RDL), calves, abs | Easy bike 20 min (or incline at 8–10%) |
| **Thu** | Push B | Overhead press, incline press, side delts, triceps | Incline walk 20 min |
| **Fri** | Pull B | Back thickness, traps, rear and side delts, arms, neck | Incline walk 20 min |
| **Sat** | Legs B | Deadlift, glutes, single-leg, hamstrings, calves, core | Easy bike 15–20 min or skip |
| **Sun** | **Rest** | Normal walking only. Weekly review and measurements. | — |

## 2. Effort: RIR (Reps In Reserve)
**RIR = how many more good reps you could have done.** RIR 2 means you stopped with 2 clean reps left. Most hypertrophy happens in the last ~5 reps before failure, so sets must be **hard**. But going to failure on big barbell lifts adds fatigue and injury risk without adding much growth.

| Block week | Compounds (bench, squat, DL, OHP, rows, pull-ups) | Isolations (laterals, curls, extensions, leg curl...) |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | RIR 3 | RIR 2 |
| Week 2 | RIR 2–3 | RIR 1–2 |
| Week 3 | RIR 2 | RIR 1 |
| Week 4 | RIR 1–2 | RIR 0–1 |
| Week 5 | RIR 1 | RIR 0 (last set to failure is OK) |
| **Week 6: deload** | RIR 4–5, half the sets | RIR 3–4, half the sets |

> [!TIP]
> Most people underestimate how many reps they have left. Once every few weeks, take the **last set of an isolation exercise** (curl, lateral raise, leg extension) to true failure and count how many reps you thought you had left versus how many you actually got. It calibrates your RIR judgment. Never do this on squats, deadlifts or bench without safeties.

## 5. Progressive overload system

### 5.1 Double progression (used for every exercise)
1. Each exercise has a rep range (e.g. 8–12) and a target RIR.
2. Use the same weight on all sets until **every set reaches the top of the range** at the target RIR.
3. Then add weight and drop back to the bottom of the range.

**Example: incline DB press 3 × 8–12, 26 kg dumbbells**

| Week | Reps (sets 1/2/3) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12 / 10 / 9 | Same weight |
| 2 | 12 / 11 / 10 | Same weight |
| 3 | 12 / 12 / 11 | Same weight |
| 4 | 12 / 12 / 12 | **Next session: 28 kg** (expect around 9 / 8 / 8) |

### 5.2 Load jumps
| Exercise type | Jump when the top of the range is hit |
|---|---|
| Squat, deadlift, hip thrust | +5 kg (+2.5 kg once progress slows) |
| Bench, OHP, rows, incline barbell | +2.5 kg (use 1.25 kg fractional plates if your gym has them) |
| Dumbbell exercises | Next dumbbell (+1–2 kg per hand) |
| Cable and machine | Next pin. For laterals, push reps up to 20 before moving the pin. |

### 5.3 Pull-up progression ladder
1. **0–2 strict reps:** assisted-pull-up machine at 3 × 5–10. When all sets hit 10, reduce assistance by 5 kg. Finish with 1 set of 3 × 5-second negatives (jump to the top, lower slowly).
2. **3–5 strict reps:** bodyweight sets at RIR 1 (e.g. 4, 3, 3), then finish with assisted reps to reach ~20 total.
3. **6–10 strict reps:** 3 × 6–10 bodyweight with double progression.
4. **3 × 10 strict:** add weight with a dip belt (+2.5 kg) and work 3 × 5–8.

Every kilo of fat you lose makes pull-ups easier, so this lift will improve faster than anything else.

### 5.4 What counts as progress in a deficit
- More reps at the same weight and RIR, **or** more weight for the same reps.
- The same performance at a lower bodyweight (your relative strength went up).
- In Phases 3–4, **holding** strength is a success.

### 5.5 Stall rule
If a lift hasn't progressed for **3 straight weeks** (each lift is trained once a week):
1. Check your sleep, protein and how much calories have dropped.
2. Cut the load by 10% and build back up over 2–3 weeks (a "reset").
3. If it stalls again, change the variation at the start of the next block (e.g. barbell bench → DB bench, back squat → hack squat).

## 6. Blocks, deloads, warm-up and cardio integration

### 6.1 Six-week blocks
**5 build weeks + 1 deload week** (weeks 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48).

**Deload week:**
- Same exercises, **half the sets**.
- Same weight at RIR 4–5, or −10% weight at your usual reps.
- Steps and incline walking stay the same.
- Weeks 12, 24, 36 and 48 are also **diet-break weeks** (maintenance calories, see the nutrition file).

**Take an early deload if two or more of these happen:** performance drops on several lifts for 2 sessions in a row · joint aches that linger · resting heart rate up > 7 bpm for 3 days · disturbed sleep · loss of motivation for more than a week.

### 6.2 Warm-up (≈ 8–10 min; your walk to the gym is the general warm-up)

**Upper days:** 15 band pull-aparts, 10 band dislocates or wall slides, 10 arm circles each way, 10 scapular push-ups, 10 face pulls (light).
**Lower days:** 10 bodyweight squats, 10 leg swings per direction per leg, 8 cat-camels, 10 glute bridges, 30 s ankle rocks per side, 5 hip airplanes per side (hold support).

**Specific ramp-up for the first compound:**

| Set | Load | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empty bar | 10 |
| 2 | ~40% of working weight | 8 |
| 3 | ~60% | 5 |
| 4 | ~75% | 3 |
| 5 | ~85–90% | 1–2 |

Second compound: 1–2 ramp sets. Isolations: one light set of 10–15 reps.

### 6.3 Cardio integration (details in the cardio file)
- Do incline walking **after** lifting, never before. You lift fresh, and the walking becomes your cool-down.
- On **leg days**, swap incline walking for **easy cycling** or a lower incline (8–10%) to reduce calf and hamstring fatigue.
- Walking hardly interferes with muscle growth. The "interference effect" mostly comes from high-volume, high-intensity running.

## 7. Phase-by-phase training adjustments

| Phase | Changes |
|---|---|
| **1 (Wk 1–8)** | Program exactly as written. Week 0 = testing. Blocks 1–2 focus on **technique** for squat, deadlift and OHP. Start at RIR 3. |

| **3 (Wk 29–42) V-taper specialization** | Add **3 sets of lateral raises at the end of both leg days** (side delts → ~19 sets/week). +2 sets each of curls and triceps. **Cut** leg press and leg extension to 2 sets each. Optional: swap barbell row for a lat-biased row (elbows close to the body, pulling toward the hip). |
| **4 (Wk 43–52)** | Keep the heavy loads. If recovery drops (sleep, mood, lifts falling), **remove 1 set from each isolation exercise** first. Never drop the main compounds. |
| **Year 2 (lean gain)** | Volume climbs toward 15–20 sets/week on priority muscles. New rep-range blocks. |

## 8. Recovery
- **Sleep 7.5–9 h** in a dark, cool room with a consistent wake time. Sleep loss during a diet increases muscle loss and hunger.
- **Protein spread** across 4–5 meals, 35–50 g each (the meal plan does this).
- **Sunday off** from lifting. Walking is fine.
- **Soreness:** if a muscle is still very sore when it's due to be trained again, keep the same weight and sets and don't add any. Soreness doesn't measure how well you trained.
- **Foam rolling, stretching, massage:** optional. They feel good but aren't required.
- **Stress:** exam periods affect recovery. Use the fallback plan below.

## 9. Fallback plans (for exams, travel, illness recovery)
Consistency over 12 months beats a perfect 6 weeks. When life gets busy:

**4-day Upper/Lower** (Mon Upper A / Tue Lower A / Thu Upper B / Sat Lower B):

## 10. Exercise technique and progression library

Progression for every exercise follows **§5 (double progression)** unless stated otherwise. **Swap** = alternative if the equipment is busy or missing.

### Push exercises
**Barbell bench press**
- *Setup:* eyes under the bar. Grip ~1.5× shoulder width (forearms vertical at the bottom). Squeeze your shoulder blades **back and down** and keep a slight arch. Feet planted and pushing into the floor.
- *Rep:* lower under control (1–2 s) to the lower chest, elbows ~45–70° from your torso. Touch without bouncing, then press up and slightly back toward your face.
- *Safety:* safety arms set just below chest height, or a spotter.
- *Progression:* +2.5 kg when 3 × 8 is hit at the target RIR.

**Incline DB press (30°)**
- Bench at 30° (higher angles shift work to the front delts). Lower the dumbbells to the line of your upper chest with elbows slightly tucked, get a deep stretch, and press up over your shoulders without banging the dumbbells together.

**Low-to-high cable fly**
- Pulleys at the lowest setting. Lock a slight bend in your elbows and **sweep your hands up and in to chin height**, squeezing the upper chest. Control the stretch on the way back.

**Standing barbell overhead press**
- Grip just outside your shoulders, wrists stacked over elbows. **Squeeze your glutes and brace your abs** (no leaning back). The bar starts on your front delts.
- Press straight up, moving your head back slightly to clear the bar, then push your head "through the window" at lockout so the bar finishes over the middle of your feet.

**Incline barbell / Smith press (30°)**
- Lower the bar to your upper chest, just below the collarbones, elbows ~45–60°. Smith machine is fine and lets you push hard safely.

**Machine chest press / pec deck**
- Set the seat so the handles line up with mid-chest. Full stretch, controlled squeeze. A safe place to push close to failure.

**DB lateral raise**
- Lean very slightly forward. **Lead with your elbows** and raise in the "scapular plane" (about 15–30° in front of your body) to shoulder height. Pinkies level with or slightly below your thumbs (don't "pour the jug"). Lower in 2–3 s. **No swinging.**
- *Progression:* reps first (up to 20), then the next dumbbell. Partial reps at the end of the last set are fine.

**Single-arm cable lateral raise**
- Cable at hand height or lowest setting, cable crossing in front of or behind your body. Raise to shoulder height. The cable keeps tension on the delt at the bottom of the movement, where dumbbells don't.

**Overhead cable triceps extension**
- Face away from the stack, rope behind your head, elbows pointing forward and kept still. Lower into a **deep stretch**, then extend fully. Training the long head in a stretched position is linked to more growth.

**Triceps pressdown / rope pressdown**
- Elbows pinned to your sides, full lockout, 2 s on the way up. With the rope, spread your hands apart at the bottom.

**EZ-bar skull crusher**
- Lower the bar toward your forehead or just behind your head, with upper arms angled slightly back. Extend without letting your elbows flare.

### Pull exercises
**Pull-up**
- Overhand grip slightly wider than your shoulders. Start each rep from a dead hang with your shoulders "set" (pulled slightly down). **Drive your elbows down toward your ribs**, bring your chest toward the bar until your chin clears it, and lower in 2 s. No kipping.

**Barbell row**
- Hinge your torso to ~30–45°, spine neutral, knees soft. Pull the bar to your lower ribs or upper abs, pause briefly, and lower under control. A little body English on the last rep is acceptable. Heaving the bar up with your hips is not.

**Single-arm kneeling cable pulldown**
- Kneel facing the high pulley. Reach up into a **full lat stretch**, then pull your elbow down and in toward your hip in an arc.

**Lat pulldown**
- Thighs locked under the pads, grip slightly wider than your shoulders, lean back ~10–15°. Pull the bar to your upper chest by driving your elbows down and back. **Full stretch at the top** on every rep.

**Chest-supported row**
- Chest stays on the pad the whole time. Row with your elbows ~30–45° from your torso, squeeze your shoulder blades, then reach forward into a stretch.

**Straight-arm cable pulldown**
- Arms nearly straight, slight hip hinge. Sweep the bar from eye level down to your thighs using your lats. Don't bend your elbows to turn it into a pressdown.

**Reverse pec deck**
- Handles at shoulder height, slight elbow bend. Sweep your arms **out and back** (think "wide", not "pinch your shoulder blades hard"). This keeps the work on the rear delts.

**Face pull**
- Rope at upper-chest to face height. Pull toward your forehead with **elbows high**, and finish with your hands rotated back like a double-biceps pose.

**Shrug**
- Straight up toward your ears, hold 1–2 s, then a full stretch at the bottom. Don't roll your shoulders. Straps are fine.

**EZ-bar curl / incline DB curl / hammer curl**
- Elbows stay still. Full range of motion, 2 s lowering, no hip swing.
- *Incline:* arms hang behind your torso for a long-head stretch.
- *Hammer:* neutral grip. It builds the brachialis and forearm, which makes the arm look thicker from the front.

**Neck flexion and extension** (lying on a bench with your head off the end)
- *Flexion:* face up, folded towel on your forehead, light plate held on top. Nod your chin toward your chest.
- *Extension:* face down, plate on the back of your head. Lift your head up.
- **Weeks 1–2: bodyweight only.** Then 2.5 kg, adding about 1.25 kg every 2–3 weeks. Slow, smooth reps through a comfortable range. **Never jerk.**
- A neck harness is the best long-term tool.

### Leg and core exercises
**Back squat (high-bar)**
- Bar on your upper traps. Feet shoulder-width apart, toes out 15–30°. **Brace:** big breath into your belly, abs tight all around.
- Sit down between your hips with knees tracking over your toes, and reach **at least parallel** (hip crease at knee level) if your mobility allows. Drive up while keeping your chest up.
- Safety pins set just below the bottom position.
- *Phase 1:* film your sets from the side, and add weight only when the form looks the same from rep to rep.

**Leg press**
- Feet in the middle of the platform, shoulder-width apart. Lower as deep as you can **without your lower back peeling off the pad**. Don't slam your knees into lockout.

**Romanian deadlift**
- Soft knees. Push your hips back and let the bar slide down your thighs while your spine stays neutral. Stop at maximum hamstring stretch (usually mid-shin), then drive your hips forward. It's a hinge, not a squat.

**Conventional deadlift**
- Bar over the middle of your feet, hip-width stance, grip just outside your legs. Push your hips back until your shins touch the bar, chest proud, **lats tight ("squeeze oranges in your armpits")**, and pull the slack out of the bar.
- Push the floor away and finish by squeezing your glutes, without leaning back. Lower under control and reset between reps in Phase 1.
- Use straps or a hook grip when grip limits you. A belt is optional from Phase 2.

**Bulgarian split squat**
- Rear foot on a bench with laces down. Place your front foot far enough forward that the heel stays down. Lean your torso slightly forward to work the glutes more, and lower until your rear knee nearly touches the floor.
- If balance limits you, hold a rack or do them in a Smith machine.

**Leg extension**
- Knee lined up with the machine's pivot, pad on your lower shin. Squeeze for 1 s at the top and lower in 2–3 s.

**Leg curl (seated preferred)**
- Hips pressed into the pad. Full range of motion with a controlled lowering. The seated version trains the hamstrings in a lengthened position, which is better for growth.

**Hip thrust**
- Upper back on the bench, padded bar over your hip crease, feet placed so your shins are vertical at the top. Chin tucked, ribs down, **full lockout with a 1 s squeeze**.

**Standing / seated calf raise**
- Full stretch at the bottom with a **1–2 s pause** (no bouncing), then rise as high as you can.
- Your walking already works your calves, so the progress comes from the pause and the full range.

**Cable crunch**
- Kneel holding the rope by your head. Curl your ribs toward your pelvis by bending your spine. Your hips stay still.

**Hanging knee / leg raise**
- Curl your pelvis up (posterior tilt) instead of just lifting your knees, and don't swing. If your grip gives out first, use the captain's chair.

**Ab wheel rollout**
- Start from your knees, ribs down, pelvis tucked. Roll out only as far as you can **without your lower back sagging**. Progress the range before adding reps.
